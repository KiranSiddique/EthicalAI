## 📌 About This Project

**EthicAI** is a fully responsive, single-page awareness website built as part of a Capstone & Professional Practices project at university level. It was developed in response to a documented gap: **80.4% of university students use AI tools daily**, yet the majority have received zero formal training on ethical AI use, data privacy, or cybersecurity risks.

This platform was designed, developed, and deployed entirely by students — for students.

---

## 🎯 Problem We're Solving

Our primary survey of **107 university students** revealed:

- 🔴 **80.4%** use AI tools daily or several times a week
- 🔴 **41.1%** have entered their full name or student ID into AI chatbots
- 🔴 **57%** have entered exam or assignment questions into AI platforms
- 🔴 **Only 24.3%** always disclose AI use to their instructors
- 🔴 **27.1%** received zero institutional guidance on AI use in projects
- 🔴 **Only 28%** are familiar with IEEE/ACM professional codes of ethics

The problem is not student bad intent — it is an **institutional policy gap**.

## ✨ Key Features

- **Awareness Hub** — Interactive educational content on ethical AI, data privacy, and cybersecurity
- **Ethics Checker** — Self-assessment tool aligned with IEEE/ACM ethical standards
- **How-To Guides** — Step-by-step guides on disclosing AI use, verifying output, protecting data
- **Policy Templates** — Ready-to-use AI disclosure statement templates (PECA & GDPR aligned)
- **Survey Module** — Built-in data collection used for our capstone research
- **Fully Responsive** — Works on mobile and desktop (student audience is mobile-first)

## ⚖️ Legal & Ethical Framework

This platform is grounded in:

- 🇵🇰 **PECA 2016** — Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (Pakistan)
- 🇪🇺 **GDPR** — General Data Protection Regulation (EU)
- 📘 **IEEE Code of Ethics** (2020)
- 📗 **ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct** (2018)
- 📙 **BCS Code of Conduct** (2022)
- 🔐 **IPR / Copyright Law** — Intellectual Property Rights in AI-generated content

## 📚 Research Foundation

Built on a systematic literature review of **15 peer-reviewed research papers** (2024–2026) from:

- arXiv
- ScienceDirect / Elsevier
- Springer
- MDPI
- PMC (PubMed Central)
- Frontiers in Education
- Taylor & Francis

Key papers include longitudinal studies, systematic reviews, and empirical surveys covering AI governance, academic integrity, cognitive over-reliance, and privacy harm frameworks.
