Step 1 — Understand What AI Can & Cannot Do

🤖

AI tools generate text by predicting patterns — they do not "know" facts, they cannot reason, and they can be confidently wrong.

1
AI is a language model — it predicts the most likely next word, not the most accurate one. It does not understand your question.
2
AI hallucinations are real — it invents citations, statistics, and facts that sound real but are fabricated. Always verify from original sources.
3
Use it to explore, not conclude — treat AI output as a starting point for your own research, never a final authoritative answer.
4
Your critical thinking is irreplaceable — the ability to evaluate AI output is itself a core professional skill employers value.

Step 2 — Check What Data You're Sharing

🔐

Every query you send may be stored and used to train future AI models. Be deliberate about what you share.

1
Never share personal identifiers — CNIC, student ID, passwords, phone numbers, or email addresses.
2
Avoid inputting exam questions — this may violate academic integrity policy and leaks confidential institutional content externally.
3
Avoid institutional data — internal documents, research files, or colleague information should never be shared with commercial AI tools.
4
Pakistan's PDPB 2023 gives you rights over your data — but only if you exercise them consciously by checking platform data policies.

Step 3 — Evaluate AI Output Critically

🔍

AI is a first draft, not a final source. Treat every AI response as unverified until you personally check it.

1
Verify all facts using Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, or your library database before citing them in any work.
2
Check all references — AI often generates plausible but non-existent citations. Confirm each one actually exists before use.
3
Apply your own judgment — does the AI's answer make logical sense? Does it align with what your course material and instructors say?
4
Add your own analysis — using AI as a springboard and then contributing original critical thought is genuinely ethical and valuable usage.

Step 4 — Disclose & Cite AI Use Properly

📝

Submitting AI-generated content without disclosure is academic misconduct — even if completely unintentional.

1
Check your institution's AI policy — what level of AI assistance is permitted in your specific course or assignment?
2
Disclose in an acknowledgment note — e.g., "AI tools were used to assist with drafting; all content was reviewed and edited by the author."
3
Cite using APA 7 format: OpenAI. (2024). ChatGPT (Version X) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/
4
ACM Principle 1.3 mandates honesty and trustworthiness — disclosure is a professional responsibility, not merely a course rule.

Step 5 — Read the Platform's Privacy Policy

📄

Each AI platform has different data policies. Some store your conversations indefinitely. Others use them to train future models.

1
OpenAI / ChatGPT — by default conversations may be used to improve models. Opt out via Settings → Data Controls → Improve the model.
2
Google Gemini — conversations reviewed by humans by default. Review your Google account Activity Controls to limit this.
3
GitHub Copilot — code may be based on public repositories. Sensitive institutional or proprietary code should never be used with Copilot.
4
GDPR & PDPB 2023 grant rights to request deletion of personal data held by third parties — including AI companies. Know your rights.

Step 6 — Keep Your Own Thinking Central

🧠

Education is about developing your thinking. AI should amplify your capabilities — never replace them or your voice.

1
Form your own opinion first — before asking AI, spend 5 minutes writing what you already know and think about the topic.
2
Use AI to challenge your ideas — ask it to argue the opposite side, spot weaknesses in your argument, or suggest additional sources.
3
Rewrite AI outputs in your own words — even using AI as a starting point, the final work must reflect your voice, judgment, and understanding.
4
IEEE's professionalism principle states AI is a tool to augment human competence — not a replacement for professional or academic judgment.
Understanding the Challenge

Two Gaps We Must Close

The rapid rise of generative AI has created two urgent, largely unaddressed challenges for students and institutions alike.

🎓

The Ethical Gap — Academic Integrity

Students frequently submit AI-generated content without disclosure or citation, leading to unintentional plagiarism and a measurable decline in critical thinking. A 2023 Stanford survey found over 60% of students used AI for assignments without understanding institutional disclosure policies.

ACM Principle 1.3 — Be Honest & Trustworthy
🛡️

The Security Gap — Data Privacy

Students routinely share personal data, exam questions, and institutional information with commercial AI platforms governed by third-party retention policies most have never reviewed. Pakistan's PDPB 2023 and GDPR both classify this as a significant and addressable privacy risk.

IEEE Article 1 — Safety, Health & Welfare

🔒

What is PII (Personally Identifiable Information)?

PII includes your name, CNIC, student ID, phone, location, or email. Any combination of data that identifies you must never be shared with AI platforms.

📋

What counts as academic misconduct?

Submitting AI-generated content as your own work without disclosure is academic dishonesty under most institutional policies — even if unintentional. Always check your course AI policy.

🌍

Your Rights Under Pakistan's PDPB 2023

Pakistan's Personal Data Protection Bill gives citizens rights to access, correct, and request deletion of personal data held by third parties — including AI companies.

⚡

Data Retention Warning

Many AI platforms retain conversation data for months or indefinitely unless you manually delete it. Check ChatGPT, Gemini, and other tools' settings pages regularly.

Secure AI Usage Guide

8 Rules for Safe AI Use

Evidence-based guidelines every student should follow when interacting with any AI platform.

01

Never Share Identifiable Information

Do not input your student ID, CNIC, phone number, or email into any AI platform. These systems retain data under terms most users have never reviewed.

02

Always Disclose AI Use in Submissions

If you use AI to assist with academic work, cite it clearly. Most institutions require disclosure. Hiding AI use is a form of misconduct under ACM Principle 1.3.

03

Read the Privacy Policy First

Before using any AI for sensitive tasks, check how inputs are stored and whether they're shared with third parties. OpenAI, Google, and others all differ significantly.

04

Verify All AI-Generated Content

AI hallucinates — it produces confident-sounding but incorrect information. Verify all claims against peer-reviewed sources before any academic use.

05

Do Not Share Exam Content

Entering exam or assignment questions into AI platforms may violate academic integrity policy and leaks confidential institutional content to external servers permanently.

06

Use AI to Learn, Not Replace Thinking

AI is most valuable for exploring ideas and getting feedback — not for generating final submissions. Build your own analytical voice and judgment over time.

07

Be Cautious with Institutional Data

Never share university credentials, internal documents, or research data with commercial AI. This may violate both institutional policy and Pakistan's PDPB 2023.

08

Know Your Rights Under PDPB 2023

Pakistan's Personal Data Protection Bill gives you rights over your data. AI platforms may not comply — know your rights and exercise them before sharing anything sensitive.
